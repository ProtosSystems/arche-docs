# Rate limits

Rate limits are applied **per organization**, not per API key. Issuing more keys
spreads work across services; it does not raise your throughput. Each key also
carries the same per-key ceiling, so one runaway worker cannot starve the rest
of your integration.

## Response Headers

Every response carries your current budget, whether or not it succeeded:

<Properties>
  <Property name="X-RateLimit-Limit" type="integer">
    Requests allowed in the current window for this endpoint class.
  </Property>
  <Property name="X-RateLimit-Remaining" type="integer">
    Requests left in the current window.
  </Property>
  <Property name="X-RateLimit-Reset" type="unix timestamp">
    When the current window resets.
  </Property>
  <Property name="Retry-After" type="seconds">
    Sent on `429` only. Wait this long before retrying.
  </Property>
</Properties>

Read these rather than inferring your budget from failures. `Retry-After` is
authoritative on a `429` — prefer it over a fixed backoff schedule.

## What Your Plan Allows

Limits differ by endpoint class, because a modeling or ingestion call costs far
more to serve than a read. All windows are 60 seconds.

| Plan | Read | Modeling | Ingestion | MCP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | 60 | 30 | — | — |
| Developer | 300 | 150 | 30 | 150 |
| Growth | 1,200 | 600 | 150 | 600 |
| Scale | 3,000 | 1,500 | 600 | 1,500 |
| Enterprise | 10,000 | 5,000 | 2,000 | 5,000 |

Reads cover company resolution, statements, filings, coverage, and
point-in-time queries — the bulk of most integrations. Negotiated agreements
may carry different limits; your entitlement is authoritative and is reported
in the developer portal.

  <p>

    <code>403 FEATURE_NOT_AVAILABLE</code> rather than an empty result, with{' '}
    <code>details.required_tier</code> naming the plan that includes it.
  </p>
</Note>

## 429 Handling

When your client is throttled, the API returns HTTP `429` with a `Retry-After`
header and the standard [error envelope](/errors).

Recommended handling:

1. stop issuing new requests for that endpoint class
2. wait for `Retry-After` seconds
3. resume with bounded concurrency rather than a burst retry

A `429` is a backoff signal, not a failure. Do not treat it as a permanent
error, and do not retry immediately — that consumes the next window before your
own work reaches it.

## Client Strategy

- Watch `X-RateLimit-Remaining` and slow down before you are throttled.
- Prefer sequential or modestly concurrent pagination unless you have measured
  headroom.
- Cache stable lookup results such as company resolution.
- Separate interactive traffic from large historical extraction jobs, so a
  backfill cannot throttle a user-facing request.

## Response Cost

Routes differ in how much work one request represents, which matters more than
request count when planning a job.

- A metric time series covers a company's whole reporting history in one
  request. It resolves in a fixed number of queries regardless of how many
  years it spans, so requesting a longer history costs little more than a
  shorter one. Prefer one wide request over a year-by-year loop.
- Listings return metadata rather than payloads. Narrative disclosure listings
  carry each disclosure's `char_count` instead of its text, because a single
  filing can hold hundreds of kilobytes across its text blocks. Read the text
  one disclosure at a time, and use `char_count` to decide what is worth
  fetching.
