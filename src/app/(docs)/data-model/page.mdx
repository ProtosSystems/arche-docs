# Data Model

Arche organizes public-company fundamentals into a hierarchy that supports both current-state and point-in-time retrieval.

## Hierarchy

Company -> Filing -> Statement -> Version -> Facts

## Company

Represents a public issuer.

Key fields:

- `cik`
- `ticker`
- `entity_name`
- `exchange`

## Filing

Represents an SEC filing such as:

- 10-K
- 10-Q
- 8-K

Key fields:

- `accession_id`
- `form_type`
- `accepted_at`

## Statement

Normalized financial statement extracted from a filing.

Examples:

- income statement
- balance sheet
- cash flow statement

## Statement Version

Represents a specific version of a statement.

Key fields:

- `statement_version_id`
- `version_sequence`
- `accepted_at`
- `is_restated`
- `source_filing`
- `accounting_standard`

Versions are immutable.

### Accounting Standard

`accounting_standard` reports the standard a filing was prepared under, derived
from the taxonomy the filer actually tagged. It is `US_GAAP` for domestic
filers and `IFRS` for foreign private issuers filing on 20-F or 40-F, which tag
`ifrs-full` concepts.

Judge it per filing rather than per company. A filer can change standard, so a
company's history can contain both, and comparing a company's lifetime against
a single standard will misreport it.

### Fiscal Identity

A statement's `fiscal_year` and `fiscal_period` follow the filer's own annual
close, not the calendar. A company whose fiscal year ends in September reports
a September period as `FY`, and its fiscal year can differ from the calendar
year the period ends in.

Rows sharing one fiscal identity can carry different period ends, because a
quarter is sometimes labelled `FY`. Keep `statement_date` alongside the fiscal
identity when a specific period end matters.

<Tip>
  <p>
    If you only keep a company identifier and a reporting period, you may lose reproducibility. For
    auditability, preserve the <code>statement_version_id</code> whenever you materialize data
    downstream.
  </p>
</Tip>
